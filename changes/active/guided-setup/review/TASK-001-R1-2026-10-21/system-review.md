I independently checked the cause against source and a small real PTY probe: start `sh` on a fresh PTY, read its announcement, TERM/reap that direct child, restore saved termios, then inspect attributes. With `echo started; stty raw -echo; exec sleep 1`, raw attributes reappeared after parent reap/restore in **1 of 30** executions. With `stty raw -echo; echo started; exec sleep 1`, they reappeared in **0 of 30**. This supports the claimed ordering race: a still-running `stty` descendant can apply attributes after the direct shell was reaped. It does not prove absence of all possible descendant behavior. The changed fixture places the readiness announcement after terminal mutation and execs its waiting process, making the recorded pid the process whose termination the test claims to observe. The restoration assertion, pid-liveness assertion and unexpected-server-exit branch remain intact; no timeout, assertion, ignore or product ownership was weakened.
