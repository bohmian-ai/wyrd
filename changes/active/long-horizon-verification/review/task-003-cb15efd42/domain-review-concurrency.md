## Ownership and interleaving analysis

`send` publishes pending and staged-reserved counters under the sender read fence before making an item receivable (`outbox.rs:271–296`). Writer completion releases each item's originating share, while failure retains its original items and reserved count (`:642–698`). No retry re-admits its slice. The two shares sum to finite capacity; unused reservation slots are part of reserved occupancy, not an unaccounted third buffer.

Shutdown takes the sender under the write fence and waits until the tracked writer ends (`:336–349`). Only the caller winning the nonzero `pending.swap(0)` takes `staged_reserved` and releases both shares (`:353–359`). A second shutdown caller returns without touching the paired subset. Once writer exit is observed, no writer decrement races that settlement. A surviving Reservation cannot alter staged totals after the fence: late stage releases its own charged share and counts its own loss, and Drop releases only its unspent slots. Concurrent reservations/late stages may change total reserved admission, but cannot steal the subset belonging to pending abandoned items. This closes the shared ownership defect without downstream subtraction guards.

The concurrent-shutdown test (`:1251–1292`) checks once-only loss/counts and separate unused-slot ownership. Its current-thread join does not force every cross-thread interleaving; the source's single-winner/fence argument supplies the stronger ownership evidence. Graceful drain, deadline abandonment and late staging keep their prior counted-loss contract.

Per-tenant sequencing used by the R2 sentinel follows actual producers and consumers. The final replay's synchronous source send completes before its HTTP response; the next same-tenant sentinel is sent afterward. Outbox run appends in arrival order (`:530–539`), and dispatch/is_ready (`:550–620`) permits only one tenant slice in flight, prioritizing its failed slice over later waiting items. Once Scribe finishes a healthy slice, handoff iterates its original items in order (`scribe_outbox.rs:950–962`). The downstream generic writer supplies the same ordering to `accept_submissions`, whose loop accepts sequentially before SubmissionSink commits. Sentinel visibility therefore puts final durable cardinality after replay processing whether they share a batch or occupy consecutive batches.

The test uses source capacity three (two reservable items), flushes before replay, keeps both writers alive, releases its only stall and introduces no terminal failure. Replay plus sentinel fit. Seeing the old recovered ID alone would not establish this ordering; seeing the distinct sentinel does. This reasoning is specific to healthy post-drain recovery. Encoding rejection, terminal Scribe refusal, crash or deadline abandonment retains accepted loss semantics and is not transformed into guaranteed delivery.
