No new inventory lookup, physical cleanup authority, or hidden second deletion owner was introduced.

The potential overlap is real: A completes physical cleanup and commits Failed/pending; B claims cleanup while A awaits its tombstone ACK; A receives ACK and calls unleased success (`None`). SQL's unleased branch can clear B's lease. B subsequently loses its lease check or its lease-fenced failure update becomes a no-op.

This interleaving has no demonstrated material failure under the approved contract. A has already completed every physical cleanup operation successfully and received its own real deletion ACK. Therefore B is repeating work whose required result already exists. Completed-object deletion and envelope deletion accept ObjectNotFound. Upload abort accepts already Aborted/Completed/Failed rows and handles competing terminal transitions. Both attempts record the same Card-wide deletion through the existing recorder; `ObjectInventory::fold` makes any deletion dominate earlier and later completion rows, regardless of append order. B cannot clear a newer lease using its fenced terminal path, and any failure it encounters after A's completed result does not create missing cleanup work. The status remains Failed; activation locks/rechecks Pending, and explicit deletion requires Active, so neither supplies a new lifecycle category for A to erase after its Failed commit. The explicit-delete owner already uses the same physical-cleanup → deletion-ACK → unleased-success convention.

Failure/cancellation before A's deletion ACK leaves durable pending/leased cleanup for B or a future claim. Failure after ACK but before A's success update leaves unnecessary retry work, which repeats safely. A failure to transition because another attempt already changed Pending returns false and does not independently clear that other attempt's cleanup. Dead-letter exclusion in unleased success is unchanged; this review does not extend the retry policy or invent an exactly-once contract.

Consequently a guard or new claim protocol solely to prevent the observed lease overwrite is not a required correction: it would not close an established missing durable effect. No material finding is proposed, and no concurrency redesign is warranted by this patch.
